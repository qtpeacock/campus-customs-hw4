# Campus Customs Usability Improvements

Four usability improvements to the core shop: two on the front end and two on the agent/backend.

## 1. Front end: Home page photo carousel

**What we did:** The home page hero now rotates through five licensed photos of Yale athletics and campus life behind "Yale gear you'll actually wear," under a dark overlay that keeps the headline easy to read. It crossfades every 6 seconds, has pause and dot controls, credits each photographer on screen, and stays still for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

**What I added:** I wanted the website to look like it was a part of Yale Spirit instead of just a blue and white screen.

**Why it helps a Campus Customs shopper or the business:** It just makes the website more interesting and better to look at which will make people more interested.

## 2. Front end: Shop by type, with sorting

**What we did:** The Products link in the nav now opens a "Shop by type" dropdown (Hoodies, Crewnecks, Quarter-Zips, T-Shirts, Long Sleeve, Jackets & Fleece), and the Products page has category tabs with counts, a "Sort by" menu (Featured, price, and name), and a default view grouped by type. Category and sort are kept in the URL, so links and the back button work, and product pages show a Products / category / item breadcrumb.

**What I added, in my own words:** It was hard for me to look through the items and make sure that everything was correct, so I wanted to organize it more.

**Why it helps a Campus Customs shopper or the business:** This is a typical tool on shopping sites, this helps a shopper see the specific items they are looking for and organize the items they want better.

## 3. Agent/backend: Saved shopping cart and cart reminder emails

**What we did:** Shoppers can now pick a size and add items to a cart that checks live stock, saved to their account when logged in (or kept in the browser for guests and moved into the account at login). For shoppers who opt in, a cart left untouched gets one friendly reminder email, written by the agent with real prices and stock filled in by the code, saved as a draft in an email outbox that staff can preview (nothing is sent automatically).

**What I added, in my own words:** I wanted the shopping cart to be recorded in the backend and it to offer the company a chance to send out follow up emails

**Why it helps a Campus Customs shopper or the business:** Keeping items in people's shopping carts will remind them of the things they like, and follow up emails for abandoned carts my also remind customers and they will end up buying things they may have not bought if they didn't receive these additional touchpoints

## 4. Agent/backend: The chatbot can use the cart and shopping history

**What we did:** The agent got four new tools (view cart, add to cart, remove from cart, and shopping activity), so a logged-in shopper can say "add the medium to my cart" or "is my cart still in stock?" and the nav's cart badge updates right away. It asks for a size before adding, refuses sold-out sizes honestly, never adds anything on its own, and uses recent chats and the cart to make personal suggestions.

**What I added, in my own words:** I wanted the agent to be able to actually help you shop

**Why it helps a Campus Customs shopper or the business:** The agent will take some of the hassle out of shopping, allowing customers to talk to the agent to shop rather than having to do anything themselves
